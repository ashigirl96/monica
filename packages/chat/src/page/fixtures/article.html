Tide pools of the northern coast

Tide pools form where the sea leaves water behind in hollows of rock at low tide. The animals that live there have to survive heat, fresh rain and the pounding of waves, and the pools of the northern coast hold some of the richest of these communities. This article walks through the zones of a pool, the animals in each, and how to visit them without harm.

Read the field guide before you go, check the tide tables for the week, and see the notes on [bracketed] names for the local terms. If you get stuck, call the ranger station before the tide turns.

The splash zone

The splash zone is wet only from spray and the highest tides. Periwinkles and barnacles close their shells for days at a time here, waiting for the next wave to bring food and water. Lichens paint the rock in orange and black bands.[1]

The middle zone

Mussels, limpets and sea stars crowd the middle zone, which is covered and uncovered by every tide. The sea stars pry the mussels open with patient force, and the limpets return to the same scar on the rock after each feeding trip.

The low zone

Only the lowest tides of the month uncover the low zone, where anemones, nudibranchs and kelp crabs live. Walk on bare rock rather than on the animals, return any stone you turn to the way you found it, and leave before the tide comes back in.
